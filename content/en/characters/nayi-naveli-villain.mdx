## Why the "Perfect Daughter-in-Law" Is Such an Effective Villain

### Domestic horror's oldest trick

The most durable antagonists in the genre are the ones you invited in. A figure who cooks, cleans, organizes, and remembers everyone's birthday is functionally impossible to accuse without looking ungrateful — which is precisely the trap the film sets. The skeptic in the household doesn't just have to prove something is wrong; he has to prove it to people whose lives have objectively improved.

### Comedy as camouflage

The comedy and family tags aren't decoration. Humor lowers your guard, and a film that makes you laugh at a chaotic Meerut household for an hour has earned the right to make the next scene uncomfortable. When a movie is genuinely funny, the horror beats land harder because you weren't braced for them.

### The supernatural question

The fantasy and horror tags point one direction. The mystery tag points another. That tension is where the **Nayi Naveli villain** becomes genuinely interesting, because the film supports more than one reading of what's actually happening in that house.
